# Alex Cattoni's voice, as she actually writes it

Sampled 23 August 2026 from the Copy Posse emails in Marlenyi's inbox
(`hello@copyposse.com`, ~201 threads). Two read in full:

- "Someone keeps farting in my mailbox 📫" — 31 July 2026
- "I am not one of *those* people" — 7 August 2026

This is observation, not recollection. If it disagrees with a memory of her
style, the emails win.

## The rhythm

**One idea per paragraph, and most paragraphs are one line.** She almost never
writes a block. The white space is the pacing.

**Ellipses carry you between beats.** Not full stops — trailing dots, at the
end of a line, so the next line lands as a continuation.

**Parallel stacks.** Four short sentences with the same shape, one per line,
to make a point by repetition rather than by argument.

**Negate, then correct.** She states what the answer is *not*, often with a
one-word paragraph ("Nope."), and only then gives it.

**A rhetorical question she then answers herself.** Usually with a wink —
she'll ask how on earth you'd connect two unrelated things, then say she has
a formula for it.

## The person

**Self-deprecation in the middle, never at the start.** She has already made
her claim before she admits the unflattering thing — that she rambles, that
she was not naturally good on camera, that winging it did not work for her.
It buys the claim credibility instead of undercutting it.

**Scare quotes on the industry's own clichés** — "fake", "salesly",
"naturally", "winging it". She is signalling that she does not believe the
word, not that she is being clever.

**Small lowercase asides** — "lol", "trust me, I've tried", "um, er, uh".

**Emoji as punctuation at the end of a line**, one at a time, never in a row
and never mid-sentence.

## The furniture

- **Sign-off is the first name alone.** Nothing else on the line.
- **There is always a P.S.**, and it is light — a joke, a callback, a personal
  aside. It frequently uses the reader's first name.
- **The CTA is offered, not demanded**, and carries a parenthetical that lowers
  the stakes — that it is free, that it is short, that there is no catch.

## Translating it to Aari

The house voice is restrained; hers is warm and chatty. They meet at: short
lines, plain words, no adjective you would not say on the phone, and an
admission of the unflattering thing. What does not travel:

- Her emoji density. The Aari rule is easy on the emoji, and a letter to a
  seller is not a creator newsletter.
- "lol" and "um, er, uh". The equivalent in a brokerage letter is a plain
  admission, not a verbal tic.
- Her exclamation marks. Aari does not use them.

Keep the structure — the withheld opening, the one-line paragraphs, the
ellipses, the negate-then-correct, the mid-letter admission, the P.S. with
the reader's first name. Drop the costume.
