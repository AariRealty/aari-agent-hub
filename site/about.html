The story

It started with a bad experience and a better idea.

Marlenyi Paredes bought her first home before she ever sold one. That purchase changed her family's trajectory — and it also showed her how much of the process runs on assumed knowledge. Documents arrive without explanation. Deadlines pass without warning. You're expected to already understand a system nobody has walked you through.

She had spent years before that building and running businesses with her husband. That background made the gap obvious: this wasn't a knowledge problem, it was an operations problem. Nobody had built the system.

So she got licensed, and then went further — earning a broker's license rather than stopping at a sales license, specifically so she could set the standard rather than work under someone else's. Aari Realty opened as a brokerage designed around two commitments: clients who understand what is happening in their own transaction, and agents who have real support behind them instead of a logo and a split.

Today Aari is a working brokerage in Lehigh Acres serving buyers, sellers and agents across Lee and Collier County — still deliberately small, still built the same way.

Broker-Owner

Marlenyi Paredes

Broker-owner of Aari Realty, and the person accountable for every file that leaves this office. She built the brokerage, she designed its systems, and she still works directly with clients — which is the only way the standard stays real.

Her approach comes from running businesses before real estate: put people first, plan with intention, and deliver the same quality every single time regardless of the size of the deal. She's direct, she'll tell you when she disagrees with you, and she would rather lose a listing than take one at a price she can't defend.

Beyond client work, she leads Aari's agent development: mentorship, weekly coaching, and the compliance and transaction systems that make it possible for a small brokerage to run to a high standard.

The Aari Standard

Six commitments. They apply to every file, every time.

These aren't values on a wall. They're the operating rules the brokerage is run by, and the things you can hold any Aari agent to.

Explain before you ask for a signature.

Nobody at Aari sends a document without walking through what it does. If a client doesn't understand what they're signing, the agent hasn't finished their job yet.

Every file is audit-ready.

Disclosures complete, deadlines documented, signatures in order, communication logged. Not because someone might check, but because that's what protects the client and the agent when something goes sideways.

Say the hard thing early.

Overpriced listings, unrealistic budgets, properties with problems — we raise it immediately and directly. Being agreeable in month one and honest in month four costs the client money.

Fair housing is not negotiable.

We do not steer, we do not discriminate, and we do not describe people when we're describing property. Every Aari agent operates under federal, state and local fair housing law without exception.

The broker is reachable.

Clients and agents get a broker who answers, not a ticketing system. That is only possible because the brokerage stays deliberately small, which is the point.

Systems, not heroics.

Good outcomes shouldn't depend on someone remembering. Transaction coordination, checklists, and tracked deadlines mean the standard holds even on the busiest week of the year.
