I’m a Florida-licensed real estate broker, the founder of Aari Realty, and the person whose license is on every file that leaves this office.

Why I built Aari.

I bought my first home before I ever sold one. It changed my family’s trajectory — and it also showed me how much of this process runs on assumed knowledge. Documents arrive with no explanation. Deadlines pass with no warning. You’re expected to already understand a system nobody has walked you through.

Before real estate I spent years building and running businesses with my husband. So the gap was obvious to me: this wasn’t a knowledge problem. It was an operations problem. Nobody had built the system.

“If a client doesn’t understand what they’re signing, the agent isn’t finished.”

So I got licensed, and then went further — I earned a broker’s license instead of stopping at a sales license, specifically so I could set the standard rather than work under someone else’s.

What that means for you

If you’re buying or selling: you get the real numbers before you get a contract. Every document explained in plain language before you sign it. Every deadline in writing from day one. And a broker you can actually reach, because we stay small on purpose.

If you’re an agent: you get a broker who answers, mentorship that works your actual pipeline, and transaction coordination so you’re in front of clients instead of buried in paperwork.

The bar isn’t “did the paperwork get done?” It’s whether I’d stake my license on this file. That’s the question, and we answer it before anyone else has to ask.

The Aari Standard

Six commitments. Every file. Every time.

Not values on a wall. The operating rules the brokerage runs on, and what you can hold any Aari agent to.

Explain before you ask for a signature.

Nobody here sends a document without walking through what it does. If the client doesn't understand it, the agent isn't finished.

Every file is audit-ready.

Disclosures complete, deadlines documented, signatures in order, communication logged. That's what protects the client and the agent when something goes sideways.

Say the hard thing early.

Overpriced listings, unrealistic budgets, properties with problems. Being agreeable in month one and honest in month four costs the client money.

Fair housing is not negotiable.

We do not steer, we do not discriminate, and we do not describe people when we're describing property. No exceptions.

The broker is reachable.

Clients and agents get a broker who answers, not a ticketing system. That's only possible because we stay deliberately small — which is the point.

Systems, not heroics.

Good outcomes shouldn't depend on someone remembering. Coordination, checklists and tracked deadlines hold the standard on the busiest week of the year.
