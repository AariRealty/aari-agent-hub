Pricing strategy

You get one launch. The first fourteen days decide the rest.

A new listing gets its largest audience in its first two weeks — every buyer already searching your criteria sees it at once. Price it correctly and you compete for the strongest of them. Price it high to "leave room" and you spend that attention teaching the market that your home is overpriced.

Priced right

Showings concentrate early, multiple buyers see it at once, and offers arrive while the listing still reads as new. Days on market stays low, which protects your negotiating position on its own.

Priced high

Showings are thin. Buyers tour your home and buy the better-priced one down the street — your listing helped sell it. Then come the reductions, chasing a market that's already moved past you.

The reality

A home that sits and cuts price twice usually closes below what an accurate list price would have produced — after months of carrying costs, showings and disruption you didn't need.

What we won't do

We won't quote you an inflated price to win your listing and then ask for reductions three weeks later. If our recommendation is lower than what another agent told you, we'll show you the sales it's based on and you can judge for yourself. No valuation, ours included, is a guarantee of what a buyer will pay.

The process

Six steps from decision to disbursement.

01

Strategy and net sheet

We walk the home, build the comparative market analysis, and give you an estimated net sheet at three price points — so the conversation is about what you actually take home, not a list price in isolation. Then we agree in writing on price, timing, showing instructions and how compensation works.

02

Prepare the home for the camera and the buyer

You don't need a renovation. You need the handful of items that actually move perceived value, and we'll tell you which are worth your money and which to skip.

  • Declutter and depersonalize the rooms buyers photograph in their heads
  • Repairs that would otherwise surface in an inspection report and cost you leverage
  • Curb appeal — the first photo and the first impression are the same thing
  • Documentation ready up front: permits, roof and HVAC ages, wind mitigation report, HOA documents
03

Launch everywhere at once

Professional photography, the MLS listing written to be searched and read, syndication to the major consumer portals, and a coordinated social and email push timed to the same window. Full marketing plan below.

04

Showings, feedback, honest adjustment

You get a weekly update with the numbers: showings, online views, saves, and what buyers and their agents actually said. If the data says something needs to change, we tell you in week two, not week eight.

05

Offers and negotiation

Price is one term of several. We evaluate every offer on the full picture and present it plainly, then negotiate from there.

  • Financing type and the strength of the buyer's pre-approval
  • Escrow deposit amount and how quickly it becomes non-refundable
  • Inspection and financing contingency windows — how long you're exposed
  • Appraisal terms and what happens if it comes in low
  • Closing date, possession, and which closing costs each side carries
06

Contract to close, tracked

Once under contract, a dedicated transaction coordinator manages the file: every deadline calendared, inspection and repair negotiations handled, appraisal and title coordinated, and your closing figures reviewed with you before closing day.

Questions sellers ask us

Honest answers to the awkward ones.

Should I sell before I buy, or buy before I sell?
It depends on your equity, your loan options, and how much uncertainty you can carry. Selling first gives you a known number and a stronger offer; buying first is more convenient and more expensive. We'll map both paths against your actual figures before you commit to either.
What repairs are actually worth doing?
Usually: paint, landscaping, anything visibly broken, and anything that will show up in an inspection and cost you leverage later. Usually not: full kitchen or bath renovations right before listing — you rarely get that money back and buyers often want their own choices anyway.
How long will it take to sell?
It varies by price range, neighborhood, condition and season. What we can give you is the current absorption rate for homes like yours — how many sell per month against how many are listed — which predicts far better than any average. Nobody can guarantee a timeline; be careful with anyone who does.
Can I sell with a tenant in the property?
Yes, though it changes the strategy. The lease generally survives the sale, which narrows your buyer pool toward investors and affects showing access. We'll review the lease and tell you honestly whether to sell occupied or wait it out.
What do I have to disclose?
Florida sellers must disclose known facts that materially affect the property's value and are not readily observable — prior water intrusion, roof and structural issues, permit problems, sinkhole activity, and the like. Full disclosure protects you: claims come from what was hidden, not from what was disclosed. General information, not legal advice.
My home didn't sell with another agent. Now what?
Expired listings almost always come down to price, presentation, access, or all three. We'll review what was done, what the market said about it, and give you a straight assessment of what needs to change. Sometimes the answer is a different price. Sometimes it's better photos and open access.
