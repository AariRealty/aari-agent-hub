Start here

You have two numbers. Most buyers only know one.

The one you'll be given

What you're approved for

A lender looks at income, debts and credit and produces a ceiling. It is a maximum, not a recommendation. In Southwest Florida that ceiling is often calculated before anyone has looked closely at what insurance, flood coverage, HOA dues and CDD assessments will actually cost on the specific home you want.

The one that matters

What you're comfortable paying

Principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, flood insurance if required, HOA or CDD, and a monthly set-aside for maintenance. This is the number that either lets you live your life or quietly takes it over. We build it with you first, then shop inside it.

Why it matters more here

In this region, insurance and flood coverage can be the difference between two homes at the same list price having very different monthly payments. Two houses, one street apart, can carry meaningfully different costs. Price alone will not tell you which one you can afford — so we don't shop on price alone.

The process

Eight steps from thinking about it to holding the keys.

01

Decide what you're actually solving for.

Before price, before neighborhoods: what is this move supposed to fix? Getting out of a rent increase. Room for a growing family. A shorter commute. Building equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage. Being closer to a school or to family.

Write it down. Every trade-off later in the process gets easier when you know which thing you refuse to give up and which things you'll flex on.

  • Must-haves: the three things you would walk away over.
  • Nice-to-haves: the things you'd pay a little more for.
  • Dealbreakers: location, condition, HOA rules, commute, flood zone.
  • Timeline: is your lease ending, a school year starting, a job beginning?
02

Build your real monthly number.

We run a full monthly estimate before you tour anything: principal and interest, property taxes at the rate that will apply after the sale, homeowners insurance, flood insurance where it applies, HOA or CDD dues, and a maintenance reserve.

Then we work backwards to a purchase price range that produces that payment — and we shop that range, not the pre-approval ceiling.

Florida detail worth knowing

Property taxes on a home you buy are frequently reassessed after the sale, so the seller's current tax bill is not a reliable forecast of yours. Homestead exemption and Save Our Homes benefits belong to the current owner and don't transfer to you. Budget from the reassessed figure, not the listing's tax history.

03

Get pre-approved — not pre-qualified.

Pre-qualification is a conversation. Pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your documents and issued a letter that a listing agent will take seriously. In a market where good homes go under contract quickly, an offer without one is usually skipped.

  • Talk to at least two or three lenders. Terms and costs vary more than people expect.
  • Compare the full cost of the loan, not only the rate quoted on the phone.
  • Ask what the letter is conditioned on, and how long it stays valid.
  • Once you're pre-approved, don't open new credit, change jobs, or move large sums without telling your lender.

Aari Realty is not a lender and does not make lending decisions or recommendations about loan terms. We're glad to share names of local lenders other clients have used; you choose your own, and we receive nothing for the introduction.

04

Choose your agent, and put it in writing.

In Florida you'll sign a written buyer representation agreement before touring homes with an agent. That's a good thing: it states what your agent owes you, what you owe them, how long it runs, and how they're paid. Read it. Ask about anything that isn't clear.

  • Ask how many buyers the agent is currently working with.
  • Ask who handles your file when they're unavailable.
  • Ask what happens to the agreement if it isn't working out.
  • Ask them to explain their compensation in plain numbers, in writing.
On compensation

Real estate commissions are fully negotiable and are not set by law, by any association, or by any multiple listing service. What a buyer's agent is paid, and by whom, is agreed in writing between you and the brokerage before you tour homes. We'll walk through ours line by line before you sign anything.

05

Shop with a filter, not with a feed.

Public search portals are useful and frequently out of date. We set up a direct MLS feed matched to your criteria so you see new listings and price changes when they happen, not days later.

On tour, we look at what the photos leave out: roof age, water heater and HVAC age, permit history on additions and lanais, flood zone and elevation, evidence of prior water intrusion, and how the property drains during a heavy rain. In this region those details drive your insurance cost and your resale, and they don't show up in a listing photo.

06

Write the offer and hold your line.

A strong offer is more than a number. It's the deposit, the closing date, the length of the inspection period, the financing and appraisal terms, and which costs each side carries. Before you sign, you'll have a written estimate of your cash to close under that exact offer.

  • Escrow deposit — the amount, who holds it, and when it becomes at risk.
  • Inspection period — how many days you have to inspect and to walk if needed.
  • Financing and appraisal contingencies — what protects your deposit and when.
  • Closing date and possession — and what happens if either side needs more time.
  • What conveys — appliances, window treatments, pool equipment, storage sheds.

We'll tell you when we think a number is too high. We'll also tell you when the home is worth stretching for. Our job is to give you an accurate read, not an agreeable one.

07

Inspection, appraisal, title and financing.

This is the stretch where deals quietly fall apart, and it's the stretch our transaction coordination exists for. Every date is calendared and tracked from the day the contract is executed.

  • Inspection — general home inspection, plus wind mitigation and four-point where insurance requires them. Add roof, pool, septic or well inspections as the property calls for.
  • Repair negotiation — a decision about which findings matter and which are noise, made against your inspection deadline.
  • Appraisal — ordered by your lender. We prepare for the possibility it comes in under contract price and what your options are if it does.
  • Title and survey — title search, lien and permit review, HOA estoppel, and survey where applicable.
  • Insurance — bind your homeowners and, where required, flood coverage well before closing. In this market that step needs a head start.
  • Final loan approval — underwriting conditions cleared and a clear-to-close issued.
08

Walkthrough, closing, keys.

You'll review your closing figures before closing day, not at the table. We walk the property with you shortly before closing to confirm agreed repairs were completed and the condition matches the contract. Then you sign, funds are disbursed, and the deed records.

  • Bring government-issued photo ID and your wire confirmation.
  • Verify wire instructions by calling the title company at a number you already have. Never trust wire details sent by email.
  • Set up utilities to transfer on the closing date.
  • File for homestead exemption if the home will be your primary residence.

Down payment assistance

Florida Housing and several county and city programs offer down payment and closing cost assistance for buyers who meet income and first-time buyer requirements. Availability and funding change throughout the year. If you might qualify, say so early — some programs require you to apply before you're under contract.

A fair question

Do you need an agent to buy a house?

No. Here is the honest comparison so you can decide for yourself.

Going it alone

  • You negotiate directly against a licensed agent who represents the seller's interests, not yours.
  • You track every contract deadline yourself. Missing one can put your deposit at risk.
  • You decide alone which inspection findings are serious and which are cosmetic.
  • You interpret disclosures, HOA documents, permit history and title issues on your own.
  • You may still be responsible for negotiated compensation depending on the contract.

With Aari representing you

  • A licensed agent whose legal duty runs to you, with a broker accountable behind them.
  • Every deadline calendared and tracked by a dedicated transaction coordinator.
  • A read on which findings are worth negotiating and which are normal for the age and area.
  • Local knowledge of flood zones, insurance, permits, assessments and HOA rules.
  • Compensation agreed in writing, in plain numbers, before you tour a single home.
Questions buyers ask us

The ones that come up every week.

How much do I need saved to buy here?

Less than most people assume, and more than the down payment alone. Plan for the down payment plus closing costs, prepaid taxes and insurance, and your inspections. Several loan programs allow 0% to 3.5% down, and closing costs are sometimes negotiable with the seller. We'll build a written estimate for your specific situation before you tour.

Is my credit score good enough?

Different programs have different standards, and the answer changes with the rest of your file. The useful move is to talk to a lender early rather than assume. If you're not quite there, a lender can usually tell you exactly what to fix and roughly how long it takes — and that's a far better plan than waiting and hoping.

How long does the whole process take?

Once you're under contract, financed purchases in this market commonly close in about 30 to 45 days, depending on your lender and the property. The search itself varies far more — some buyers find the right home in two weeks, others take six months. Being fully pre-approved and clear on your criteria is what shortens it.

Do I really need flood insurance?

It depends on the property's flood zone and your lender's requirements. In parts of Lee and Collier County it is required; in others it's optional but often still worth carrying. We check the flood zone and elevation on every home you seriously consider, before you write an offer, because it changes your monthly payment.

What's the deal with buying new construction?

The on-site sales representative works for the builder. You can still have your own agent represent you — in most cases at no additional cost to you — but you generally need to bring that agent with you on your first visit. Bring us before you register, not after, and we'll review the builder's contract, which is written to protect the builder.

Should I wait for rates to come down?

Nobody can honestly tell you where rates go next, and any agent who does is selling you something. What we can do is show you what waiting costs and what buying now costs, side by side, in your actual numbers. Then it's your decision, made with real figures instead of a headline.
