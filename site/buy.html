Everything that happens between now and your keys.

Start here

You have two numbers. Most buyers only know one.

What you’re approved for

A lender reviews income, debts and credit and produces a ceiling. It’s a maximum, not a recommendation — and it’s usually calculated before anyone has priced the insurance, flood coverage, HOA dues or CDD assessment on the specific home you want.

What you’re comfortable paying

Principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, flood where required, HOA or CDD, and a monthly maintenance set-aside. This is the number that either lets you live your life or quietly takes it over. We build it first, then shop inside it.

Why it matters more here

In this region, insurance and flood coverage can be the difference between two homes at the same list price carrying very different monthly payments. Two houses one street apart can cost meaningfully different amounts to own. Price alone won’t tell you which one you can afford — so we don’t shop on price alone.

The process

Eight steps from thinking about it to holding the keys.

01

Decide what you’re actually solving for

Before price, before neighborhoods: what is this move supposed to fix? Getting out from under a rent increase. Room for a growing family. A shorter commute. Building equity instead of paying someone else’s mortgage.

Write it down. Every trade-off later gets easier when you know which thing you refuse to give up and which you’ll flex on.

  • Must-haves: the three things you’d walk away over
  • Nice-to-haves: what you’d pay a little more for
  • Dealbreakers: location, condition, HOA rules, commute, flood zone
  • Timeline: lease ending, school year starting, job beginning
02

Build your real monthly number

We run a full monthly estimate before you tour anything: principal and interest, property taxes at the rate that will apply after the sale, homeowners insurance, flood where it applies, HOA or CDD dues, and a maintenance reserve. Then we work backwards to a price range that produces that payment.

Florida detail worth knowing

Property taxes are frequently reassessed after a sale, so the seller’s current bill is not a reliable forecast of yours. Homestead exemption and Save Our Homes benefits belong to the current owner and don’t transfer to you. Budget from the reassessed figure.

03

Get pre-approved — not pre-qualified

Pre-qualification is a conversation. Pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your documents and issued a letter a listing agent will take seriously. In this market, an offer without one usually gets skipped.

  • Talk to two or three lenders — terms and costs vary more than people expect
  • Compare the full cost of the loan, not only the rate quoted on the phone
  • Ask what the letter is conditioned on and how long it stays valid
  • Once approved: no new credit, no job changes, no large transfers without telling your lender

Aari Realty is not a lender and makes no lending decisions or recommendations about loan terms. We’re glad to share names of local lenders other clients have used; you choose your own, and we receive nothing for the introduction.

04

Choose your agent, and put it in writing

In Florida you’ll sign a written buyer representation agreement before touring homes with an agent. That’s a good thing: it states what your agent owes you, what you owe them, how long it runs, and how they’re paid. Read it. Ask about anything unclear.

  • How many buyers is this agent currently working with?
  • Who handles your file when they’re unavailable?
  • What happens to the agreement if it isn’t working out?
  • Can they explain their compensation in plain numbers, in writing?
On compensation

Real estate commissions are fully negotiable and are not set by law, by any association, or by any multiple listing service. What a buyer’s agent is paid, and by whom, is agreed in writing between you and the brokerage before you tour homes. We walk through ours line by line first.

05

Shop with a filter, not with a feed

Public search portals are useful and frequently out of date. We set up a direct MLS feed matched to your criteria so you see new listings and price changes when they happen, not days later.

On tour we look at what the photos leave out: roof age, water heater and HVAC age, permit history on additions and lanais, flood zone and elevation, evidence of prior water intrusion, and how the property drains in a heavy rain. Those details drive your insurance cost and your resale.

06

Write the offer and hold your line

A strong offer is more than a number. It’s the deposit, the closing date, the length of the inspection period, the financing and appraisal terms, and which costs each side carries. Before you sign, you’ll have a written estimate of your cash to close under that exact offer.

  • Escrow deposit — the amount, who holds it, when it becomes at risk
  • Inspection period — how many days to inspect and to walk if needed
  • Financing and appraisal contingencies — what protects your deposit and when
  • Closing date and possession — and what happens if either side needs more time
  • What conveys — appliances, window treatments, pool equipment, sheds

We’ll tell you when a number is too high. We’ll also tell you when a home is worth stretching for. Our job is an accurate read, not an agreeable one.

07

Inspection, appraisal, title and financing

This is the stretch where deals quietly fall apart, and it’s what our transaction coordination exists for. Every date is calendared and tracked from the day the contract is executed.

  • Inspection — general, plus wind mitigation and four-point where insurance requires them
  • Repair negotiation — which findings matter and which are noise, decided against your deadline
  • Appraisal — ordered by your lender; we prepare for a low appraisal and your options
  • Title and survey — title search, lien and permit review, HOA estoppel, survey where applicable
  • Insurance — bind homeowners and flood well before closing; in this market that needs a head start
  • Final loan approval — underwriting conditions cleared and a clear-to-close issued
08

Walkthrough, closing, keys

You’ll review your closing figures before closing day, not at the table. We walk the property with you shortly before closing to confirm agreed repairs were completed and the condition matches the contract. Then you sign, funds disburse, and the deed records.

  • Bring government-issued photo ID and your wire confirmation
  • Verify wire instructions by calling the title company at a number you already have — never trust wire details sent by email
  • Set utilities to transfer on the closing date
  • File for homestead exemption if this will be your primary residence

Down payment assistance

Florida Housing and several county and city programs offer down payment and closing cost help for buyers who meet income and first-time buyer requirements. Availability and funding change through the year. If you might qualify, say so early — some programs require you to apply before you’re under contract.

Questions buyers ask us

The ones that come up every week.

Is my credit score good enough?
Different programs have different standards, and the answer changes with the rest of your file. The useful move is to talk to a lender early rather than assume. If you’re not quite there, a lender can usually tell you exactly what to fix and roughly how long it takes — a far better plan than waiting and hoping.
How long does the whole process take?
Once under contract, financed purchases in this market commonly close in about 30 to 45 days depending on your lender and the property. The search varies far more — some buyers find the right home in two weeks, others take six months. Being fully pre-approved and clear on your criteria is what shortens it.
What’s the deal with new construction?
The on-site sales representative works for the builder. You can still have your own agent represent you — in most cases at no additional cost to you — but you generally need to bring that agent on your first visit. Bring us before you register, not after, and we’ll review the builder’s contract, which is written to protect the builder.
Should I wait for rates to come down?
Nobody can honestly tell you where rates go next, and any agent who does is selling you something. What we can do is show you what waiting costs and what buying now costs, side by side, in your actual numbers. Then it’s your decision, made with real figures instead of a headline.
Can I use my own inspector and title company?
Yes. You choose your inspector, your lender and, subject to what’s negotiated in the contract, the closing agent. We’ll give you names other clients have used and tell you what to ask each one. We don’t receive anything for a referral.
