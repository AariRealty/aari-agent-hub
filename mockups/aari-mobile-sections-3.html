Sections on a phone, third set

The first six all answered “where does the row go”. These three ask a different question — whether there needs to be a row at all, and whether the bar you already have could do both jobs. The desktop rail is untouched in all three.

Option 7Stop splitting the page

The sections stop being separate pages. Deals becomes one page with Files, Review, Deadlines, Listings and Compliance stacked down it as headings you scroll past. Nothing to tap, nothing to find, nothing hidden — a bar appears at the top telling you which section you are currently in as you go.

Scroll and watch the top bar name the section
Zero navigation to learn, and nothing can be missed because everything is on the way past. Deals becomes 3.3 screens long at this card count and would be far longer with the real ones — and reaching Compliance means scrolling through four sections you did not want.
Option 8A pill in the corner

A single pill floats in the bottom-right corner, above the tab bar, carrying the name of the section you are in. Tap it and the others fan out above it. It sits over the page rather than taking a band from it, so it covers one corner of one card instead of a full-width strip, and it is the closest thing on the screen to your thumb.

Tap the dark pill, bottom right
Names your section, costs no layout height, and is the easiest thing on the screen to reach. It floats over your cards — a small permanent obstruction in the corner, and one more thing on a screen you wanted quieter.
Option 9One bar, both levels

The bar you already have does both jobs. It shows the five tabs; tap one and the bar itself becomes that tab’s sections, with a back arrow to return to the tabs. One strip of screen, two levels of navigation, no second row anywhere and nothing new added to the page.

Tap a tab at the bottom, then the back arrow
Costs nothing, covers nothing, sections at maximum size under your thumb. You cannot see the tabs and the sections at the same time — while you are in Deals, the other four tabs are behind the back arrow. And measured against all 21 of your real section names on a 390px phone, one of them does not fit: Announcements, on Reach, needs 78px in a 66px slot. That row would have to scroll sideways, or that one section needs a shorter name.

Which one I would take

Of these three, option 9 — and out of all nine it is now the one I would build. It is the only answer that costs no height, covers nothing, hides nothing behind a gesture you would have to be taught, and puts the sections at full size under your thumb. The bar is already there; it just does two jobs instead of one, and the back arrow tells you which job it is doing.

Two costs, both worth saying plainly. While you are inside Deals the bar shows Deals’ sections and not the other four tabs, so getting from Deadlines to Money is two taps rather than one — acceptable only because you move between sections far more than between tabs, there being 21 of one and five of the other. And I measured all 21 of your real section names in that bar at 390px: twenty fit, Announcements does not — it needs 78px in a 66px slot. Either that one row scrolls sideways like the shelf does today, or Announcements gets a shorter name. I would not decide that for you.

Option 7 is the most honest to how you actually read — you scroll until something catches you — and it is the only option with nothing to learn at all. I would not build it for Deals, where stacking five sections makes a page you have to scroll for a long time to get through. Option 8 is the easiest to reach and the one I would pick if you want the sections nearest your thumb, but it puts a permanent object on top of your cards, which is the thing you flagged in the first place.

Standing recommendation across all nine: 9 first, then 6, then 1. Say a number and I will build it.
