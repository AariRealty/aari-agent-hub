Three ways to handle the missing information

The section goes back to how it was — title on the left, a plain list underneath — and the four filters move to the right of the header. What changes between these three is how much the row tells you about what is not on the record. Real rows out of agent_contacts, cut to eight each so you can compare them side by side; the dropdowns work in all three.

Option ANothing on the row

The gaps live only in the filter. You pick Missing › Instagram, the list narrows to the people who need one, and you work straight down it. The row goes back to exactly what it was — tier, name, who they are, and the button.

Option BOne quiet count

A single small 6 GAPS tag where the pills used to be — one token instead of four, and no words competing with the name. Click it and the missing fields open on a line underneath, for that row only. Same tag the Today queue already uses.

Option CA completeness bar

No words at all — a short bar showing how much of the record is filled in, and how many of the eleven fields are there. You can see at a glance which records are thin without reading anything, and the filter still tells you exactly which field is missing.

Which one

I would pick A. You already know what you filtered for — if you asked for the people missing an Instagram handle, every row on screen is missing one, so printing it on each line is telling you something you just asked for. It also keeps the row honest at a glance: tier, name, who they are, and what state the relationship is in.

B is the safe middle if you want a nudge that a record is thin while you are scrolling unfiltered, and it is one tag rather than four. C is the prettiest and the least useful day to day — almost every record in the book sits at 0–5 of 11, so the bars would all look the same until the data gets filled in.
