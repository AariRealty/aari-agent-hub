Which one

Broker A. It is the true mirror of what you approved on the agent side, so the two roles read as one product rather than two, and its dials are the numbers you would quote if someone asked how the brokerage is doing. The roster line earns the top of the page because it is silently breaking things you cannot see: four wrong addresses means four agents never got the signing policy, which is exactly why two of your five posts have never been opened by anyone.

Broker B is the one to take if the question you open this screen with is “are we making money”. It is the only version where the $20,132 a year of overhead appears anywhere on the dashboard. Broker C is the most useful on a bad day and the most demoralising on a good one — five dials all counting failures, with no number on the screen that can ever be good news. I would not make it the permanent home page, but it would make a very good Needs you tab.

As before, these are twelve independent pieces. Say one per section if you want to mix — for instance A’s dials with C’s line and route.
