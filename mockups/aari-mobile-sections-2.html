Sections on a phone, second set

Three that are not variations on the first three. None of them adds a band to the screen — each one puts the sections somewhere that already exists. The desktop rail is untouched in all three.

Show me
Option 4A chip in the bar

The section goes in the empty space next to the logo, as a small chip carrying its name — Overview ▾. Tap it for the list. That strip of the top bar is dead space today, so the sections cost nothing at all: no band, no height, nothing covered, and the page still says in words where you are.

Tap the chip next to the logo
Free, permanent, and it names your section without a row. The other sections are a tap away, and the chip is small — it is the least loud of the six so far.
Option 5Swipe, with dots

No control at all — you swipe the page sideways to move between the sections of the tab you are in, the way you flick between phone home screens. A row of dots under the heading says how many there are and which one you are on. On Today that is two dots; on Deals, five.

Drag the cards sideways
The fastest way to move that exists on a phone, and it costs 12px. The dots do not name anything — you learn the order by feel, and there is no way to jump straight to the fifth.
Option 6The heading is the list

The greeting comes off, and the sections become the page heading. The one you are in is the big title; the rest sit under it in small grey. It replaces the greeting rather than adding a band, and it frees the 37px shelf at the bottom. Always starts at the same left edge, and every section is a word you can read.

Tap one of the small names
All sections visible as words, all the time, in a fixed spot, and nothing is ever covered. It is a trade, not a freebie — it takes more off the top than the greeting did and gives back the shelf at the bottom, so the first card lands near where it does today. And the greeting goes: the page stops saying good morning and starts saying Overview.

Which one I would take

Out of all six, option 6. It hides nothing, covers nothing, and never moves. Every section is a readable word in a fixed place, which is the thing that made the desktop rail the right answer — this is the same idea, folded into a heading that was already on the screen doing nothing but saying good morning. It is not free: the measurements under each phone show it starting the first card lower than option 4 or 5, because a five-name heading is taller than a greeting. What it buys for that is never needing a tap or a swipe to see what exists.

Option 4 is the close second and the safer one: the chip is permanent, names your section, costs nothing, and keeps the greeting. It is quieter than 6 but it hides four names behind a tap. Option 5 is the nicest to actually use and the worst to learn — five dots tell you nothing about what is in them, and on Deals you would be swiping four times to reach Compliance.

From the first set, 1 is still the best of those three. If you want the shortest possible answer: 6 if you want to see everything, 4 if you want the quietest screen, 1 if you want the smallest change to what is already built.
