Why it works: the prompt sits next to the thing it writes, so there is never a question of what it is for. Sections can be done out of order, and the header counts what is left. Cost: it is a long scroll on a phone, and a half-finished letter is easy to abandon. Five open cards is five decisions visible at once.

Each prompt names its format and its character limit, the same way your Canva notes do. Running them all takes about twenty seconds.

Why it works: it is the fastest path to a finished letter, one tap writes everything still missing, and the agent reads the whole thing once instead of judging five fragments. Cost: an agent who taps “run the lot” and sends without reading has just posted five machine-written paragraphs under your brokerage’s name. It is the option that most needs a forced preview before send.

Why it works: it is the closest thing to the Notes panel you showed me, one instruction, its format rule, a worked example, and answers to pick from rather than a box to fill. It is the only one where the agent reads what they are sending. Cost: five screens to write one letter, and no way to see the whole thing until the end. An agent who wants to change section one at section four has to go back.

What I would still hold back. Whatever is chosen, the letter should not be sendable straight from the last prompt. One preview screen showing the finished letter and the recipient count, 20 people, with the send button under it. That is the difference between a tool and an accident.
