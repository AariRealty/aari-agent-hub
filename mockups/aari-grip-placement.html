Where the grip goes

You are right, and it is a real bug rather than a taste question. I put the grip in the card’s top-right corner and had it measure its way clear of anything already there — but I only measured buttons, not the text labels. So on Announcements it landed on top of “3 of 5 posted”, and on Company calendar it crowded “Add to my calendar”. Nearly every card keeps something in that corner, so the corner was the wrong place from the start.

All three below are live — drag them. They use the real cards with the real busy headers, so you can see whether anything collides.

Where 1A tab on the left edge

The grip hangs on the card’s left border, half outside it, vertically centred — with the × underneath. Nothing lives on the left edge of any card, so there is nothing to measure around and nothing it can ever land on. It reads like a handle on a drawer.

Where 2In front of the title

The grip fades in at the start of the header row and nudges the title across, the way a list row does. The × goes to the far right, after everything the header already has, so it queues rather than overlaps. Both are inside the card, so nothing floats over the edges.

1 point at a card 2 drag the grip beside its name
Where 3Press and hold

No grip at all, and nothing added to any card. Press and hold a card for a moment and it lifts off the page, then move it. The only thing that appears is a small × on the corner while it is lifted. This is how a phone home screen works, and it is the only one of the three that adds nothing to the design.

Which one

Where 1, the tab on the left edge. It is the only one that cannot collide with anything, now or later — the left edge of a card is empty on every card you have and on every card you will add. The other two both depend on the header staying roughly as it is. After the mistake I just made, that matters more than looks.

Its honest cost: the tab sits half outside the card, so on the leftmost column it hangs into the margin. On a phone that margin is 11px and it will sit close to the screen edge.

Where 2 is the tidiest — everything stays inside the card and nothing floats. But it moves your card titles sideways when you point at them, which is a small animation happening on every card you pass over, and it is the one most likely to feel busy.

Where 3 is the cleanest dashboard of the three, because it adds nothing at all. The cost is that nothing tells you the feature exists — you would only find it if someone told you, and you have already spent a day not finding a button that was visible. That is the reason I am not recommending it, not the mechanics.
