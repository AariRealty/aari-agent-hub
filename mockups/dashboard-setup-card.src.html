The setup card

Placement is settled — a card of its own beside the calendar, visibly temporary, gone when you finish. This is three ways to draw what is inside it. Everything else on these three dashboards is identical and is what you have already approved.

Setup 1One step, and an arc

No list. An arc showing two of five, and the single next step beside it, in the same visual language as the doughnut you already have. It answers “how far in am I” and “what is next” and refuses to answer anything else — the quietest of the three by a distance.

Setup 2A numbered route

The five steps as a path with a line running through it — done ones are a dark disc with a tick, the one you are on is ringed in red, the rest are numbered and hollow. Two-word labels instead of sentences. It looks like a route on a map rather than a chore list, and no step is crossed out.

Setup 3The one thing in the way

It stops being a progress tracker and names the blocker: your seat is not finished because the onboarding checklist is empty. Underneath, two chips for what is already true — 207 contacts, 2 closings — so it reads as one piece of paperwork outstanding rather than as being three fifths of the way through being a real agent.

Which one

Setup 3. The old card said “2 of 5” to someone with 207 contacts, two closings and a live listing, which reads as though you are two fifths of an agent. You are not — you have one piece of paperwork outstanding. Naming that is both truer and shorter, and the two chips underneath stop the card feeling like a scolding.

Setup 1 is the one to take if you want it to disappear into the page: it is the smallest, quietest version and it borrows the doughnut’s language so it does not read as a new kind of thing. Setup 2 is the one to take if seeing the whole path matters — and it is the only one of the three where nothing is crossed out, which was the part of the old card that read as a chore list.
