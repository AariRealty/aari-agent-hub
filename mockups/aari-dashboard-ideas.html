Idea 1Today, and nothing else announcement: a line that disappears

The screen has one job: tell you what to do in the next hour. The number you owe today sits at the top at the size it deserves, the three things due sit under it, and everything else — income, listings, calendar, ramp — collapses into five small tiles you tap when you want them. Nothing on this screen is a chart of something that has not happened yet.

What earns its place

  • Today’s 3 conversations, straight off your Goal Engine, with what each is worth
  • The 816 Frederick Reid draft — closed on paper six days ago, still unsubmitted
  • The two calendar entries that are actually today

What comes off

  • Your execution — the 33% Mailer / Report / Notes card has no data behind it
  • The monthly income bars — five bars, three of them empty
  • The ramp — it is onboarding; it belongs on a first-run screen, not forever
Idea 2The funnel announcement: an inbox you clear

The dashboard becomes the shape of the business: contacts → conversations → appointments → under contract → closed, with the real count under each. It exists to show you where the line stops. On your rows it stops in the first two columns, and the screen says so out loud instead of leaving you to work it out from four separate cards.

What earns its place

  • The five stages with their real counts, so a zero is impossible to miss
  • One named blockage per screen, with the button that fixes it
  • Announcements as an inbox with two unacknowledged, not a carousel

What comes off

  • The profile card — a photo of yourself is not information
  • The month calendar grid — nobody plans a month from a dashboard
  • The goal card as a separate card; its number lives in the funnel now
Idea 3The week announcement: stamped on the day it landed

Real estate runs in weeks — your goal is a weekly number, pop-bys are a weekly number, the coaching calls are weekly. So the dashboard is one week across the top, today enlarged, with calls, tasks and the broker’s posts all sitting on the day they belong to. Under it, the only two counters that matter this week, and what is stuck.

What I would actually build

Idea 2, the funnel — because of what your rows say. You have 207 contacts and not one of them has a next action. You have zero conversations logged and zero appointments. You have a file that closed on paper on the 14th and is still sitting as a draft with no commission on it. Those four facts are the whole story of why August has no closings, and the dashboard you have today does not say any of them: they are spread across a task list, a pipeline tab, and a card that has never been true.

The funnel makes a zero impossible to hide. And it scales to the broker side without redrawing anything — the same five columns, summed across seven agents, is the report you actually need in order to know which of them to call.

Idea 1 is the one to take if what you want is calm: it is by far the quietest screen and it is the most honest about the fact that a dashboard you look at every morning only needs to answer one question. Idea 3 is the best fit for how the business actually moves, and it is the one I would pick second — but a week strip is seven columns of small text, which is the opposite of what you asked for when you said you wanted fewer things to read.

On the announcement card specifically: it is currently a carousel of five posts, and your own rows show two of the five were opened by anyone at all, with two requiring an acknowledgement that nobody has given.
