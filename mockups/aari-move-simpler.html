Three simpler ways to move a card

Fair point — what I built covers every card with a panel and puts three separate control groups on each one: arrows, a width stepper and a Hide button, plus a hint line, a Reset and a tray. That is a lot to take in to move one thing. All three below drop the width control entirely — cards keep the widths they have — and each keeps only one way to move and one way to hide.

These are live. Click and drag them. Nothing here is a picture.

Way 1A grip in the corner

No mode to turn on. Point at any card and a small grip and an × appear in its corner. Drag the grip to move it, press the × to take it off. The card is never covered, so you can still read what you are moving.

1 point at a card 2 drag the grip
Way 2A list of your sections

One button opens a small list of every section, in the order they appear. Drag a row up or down to move it, flip the switch to hide it. Your dashboard is never touched — the cards stay exactly as they are while you rearrange the list beside them.

1 press Sections 2 drag a row, or flip a switch
Way 3Two arrows and an ×

No dragging at all. Press Move and each card gets three tiny buttons in its corner — back, forward, and off. Press Done when you are finished. Identical on a phone and a laptop, and there is nothing to learn.

1 press Move 2 tap ◀ or ▶ 3 press Done

Which one

Way 1. It is the only one with no mode at all — nothing to turn on, nothing to turn off, and no state to be stuck in. Two controls, both on the card, both doing the obvious thing. It also never covers the card, which was the worst part of what I built: you lost sight of the thing you were moving.

The cost of Way 1, honestly: the grip only appears when you point at a card, so on a laptop you have to know it is there the first time. On a phone it is always visible, which is where it matters most.

Way 3 is the one to take if you would rather never drag. Dragging on a long dashboard means dragging past the fold, which is fiddly on any device. Three taps and it has moved. It is the most predictable of the three and the slowest.

Way 2 is the calmest and the most indirect. Nothing on your dashboard moves while you are deciding, which some people much prefer — but you are arranging a list of names rather than the thing itself, so you have to hold the layout in your head.

Whichever you pick, the width control goes. Four columns of choice per card was the bulk of the complexity and almost none of the value — the calendar wants to be wide, everything else does not, and that can just be true without anyone setting it.
