Your Database page, three ways to make it more useful

Same page you have — the banner, Log with no contact, the tabs, the households strip, the chips, search and sort, the serif rows with + Conv and the tier. Nothing moved. Each version adds one thing that answers who is healthy and who needs me without you reading every row.

Option 1State you can see

The smallest change. Your rows already say “Leaking, 115 days quiet” — but in grey, at the end of a sentence. Here it gets a colour dot on the left and the state word in colour, and the chips get matching dots so a filter and a row speak the same language. Everything else is untouched.

Option 2A due column

Adds one column on the right: when that person is next owed a call. Tier A is weekly, B monthly, C quarterly, so every row has a due date whether or not anyone has ever called. Turns “ranked by who is being neglected” from a promise in the subtitle into something you can read down the page.

Option 3Banded list

Keeps one list but puts a thin band between the groups — Overdue, due this week, never contacted — and a health strip under the banner that doubles as a filter. You can still scroll the whole book; you just cannot miss where the top of it ends.

Which one

I would ship 1 and 2 together — they are both small, they do not move anything, and between them a row tells you the state and the deadline without you reading the sentence. That is the whole difference between a directory and something that keeps you top of mind.

3 only earns its keep once the book is being worked. Today it would show two bands with one name each and a third with 194, which is a lot of structure for very little. Worth revisiting in a month.
