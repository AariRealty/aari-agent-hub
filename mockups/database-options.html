Three ways to build Database

Same idea as your page — everyone you know, ranked by who is being neglected — rebuilt in the dashboard’s language. Real counts: 204 clients and 3 vendors, tiers A 22 · B 76 · C 101 once households collapse, and 199 of them never touched. Filters and the conversation button work.

Option AHealth first

Leads with the one number that matters — how much of the book is going cold — as a single bar, then the filter chips, then the list. Closest to your page, with the neglect state promoted from a line of small text to the thing you see first.

Option BThree tier columns

Splits the book into A, B and C side by side, with A dark because that is the column you are meant to work. Shows the shape of the database in one glance — 22 people who should hear from you weekly next to 101 who should not.

Option CA worklist, not a directory

Drops the idea of browsing everyone. Shows only what needs attention today with the rest behind a count, and puts the household merge and the untiered contacts up as jobs. Least like a spreadsheet, most like a to-do.

Which one

I would pick A. Your page already says the right thing — ranked by who is being neglected — but that fact is buried in grey text at the end of each row; the bar puts it at the top where it changes what you do next. B is the best for deciding where to spend an hour, and the only one that makes the 22 / 76 / 101 shape visible. C is the most opinionated and the one I would build second, once the database is actually being worked — right now it would show 199 people and no useful sort.
