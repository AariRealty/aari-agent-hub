Who do you call?

You said picking a main contact is confusing, and you are right — “main contact” is my words, not yours, and it never says what it actually changes. All it decides is two things: whose name the household shows under in your list, and whose number the call button uses. Three ways to handle that, on three of your real households.

Why these three. The Becs are easy — Rolando has no number on file, so there is nothing to decide. The Bushes are a genuine coin toss — Aaron and Patty both have a phone, both an email, both tier B, neither ever contacted. The Capestanys sit in between. If a design only feels clear on the Becs it has not solved anything.

Option AAlready decided

You are never asked. The system picks whoever it can actually reach and tells you why in one line. If it guessed wrong, one tap swaps it. On the thirteen households you have waiting, this is thirteen fewer decisions.

Option BOne plain question

The words main contact disappear. It asks “when you call, who picks up?” and shows you both people with the facts that matter — phone, email, tier, last spoken to. You answer a question about your own business, not a database field.

Households
Option CNo main at all

The household keeps both names, always — “Aaron and Patty Bush.” There is no main person to choose. The only thing you set is which number the call button dials, and where there is only one number it is already set.

Which one

I would pick C. It deletes the confusion rather than explaining it — there is no main contact to misunderstand, because the household is the contact. Both names show, so you never open a card wondering where the husband went, and the one real decision left is the one you would actually make out loud: which number do I dial. On nine of your thirteen pairs there is only one working number anyway, so nine of them decide themselves.

A is the fastest to get through — thirteen households cleared without answering anything — but it is guessing on the Bushes and you would have to notice and correct it. B is the clearest single screen and the one to take if you want to be asked deliberately, but it is still thirteen questions, and on the Bushes it asks you something you may not have an answer to yet.
