Same idea — the card holds the things that arrive on a schedule, so it never empties and it reads the same at six agents or six hundred. Three different designs for it. Needs you is the ledger you picked, and Who to call is Score A, in all three.

Coming 1A ruler

The next sixty days as a single line across the card, with a marker at each date, spaced by how far away it actually is. You see the shape of the next two months in one glance — whether things are bunched or spread — which a list cannot show. A red tick marks today.

Coming 2Next up, then the rest

One event gets the whole top of the card in black — the number of days, whose it is, and what it needs — and the rest sit under it as thin one-line rows. It answers “what is next” before it answers “what is coming”, which is the order you actually need them in.

Coming 3By month

Grouped under month headings with a count on each: August 1, September 2, October 2. It is the only one of the three that still works when the list is long — at a hundred agents you would read “September · 9” and open the month rather than scrolling names. The nearest month carries a dark count so it stands out.

Which one

Coming 3, by month. You asked what this slot is worth at a hundred agents, and month grouping is the only one of the three that answers it: the card stops being a list of names and becomes a set of counts you can open. At six agents it reads as five names under three headings; at six hundred it reads as “September · 48” and does not grow.

Coming 2 is the better card for today, honestly — there are five things in the next two months and the only one that matters this week is Milennys on the 30th, which is exactly what it puts in black. Take it if you want the dashboard to nag you about the next one rather than show you the shape. Coming 1 is the most interesting to look at and the one I would drop first: a ruler is lovely with five markers and unreadable with fifty, and it is the only version where two events on nearby dates overlap each other.

One thing to say again, because it decides whether this card can exist at all: start_date is null on all seven agents. Licence renewals have no column in the Hub either. Whichever design you pick, the card cannot be built with real dates until those two are filled in — and the fact that neither exists is probably the more useful finding.
