The broker, in the agent’s shape

I measured the agent dashboard before drawing anything. Its shape is two bands: four cards across the top, then the calendar taking three columns with a single tall card standing beside it in column four. That is the whole thing — 0% leftover white, 716px of grid. The broker cannot be two bands, because it carries two cards the agent does not have: Who to call and Needs you. Those two are what force a third band. So the question is not whether to copy the agent’s shape — it is where those two extra cards go.

What the three below change

All three pull the calendar back to three columns, exactly as the agent has it, and put a tall card beside it. They differ only in which card stands there and where the other two land. Everything above the calendar stays as it is — your photo, deal volume, the ring, Today — because you approved that row.

Each frame carries two numbers, because one is not enough and I have already been caught by that once. The first is gaps between cards. The second is how much of a row the text actually fills inside Who to call — a card can score perfectly on the first and still be mostly empty.

One caveat about those live figures. The preview frames below are narrower than your real screen — 1084px against 1254 — which compresses the gap figure and flattens the difference between the three. So I also built all three inside the actual hub at full width and measured them there. Those are the numbers in the table, and they are the ones the recommendation rests on. Which one

Read the measured numbers under each frame first. My opinion is below them, and it is worth less than they are.

Option C. It is the closest of the three to what the agent view actually is, and it is the only one that puts your call list where your eye lands. Three measured reasons:

— The text fills 88% of each row in Who to call, against 51% in A and B and 51% in the arrangement built this morning. In a narrow column the name and the reason stack; across two columns half of every line is empty air. This is the number that matters most, because it is white inside a card, which is the kind you actually notice.

— It makes the shortest page — 1630px against 1662 as built and 1754 for A. Less scrolling to reach the bottom of the dashboard.

— Who to call moves up a band. Today tells you what is due; Who to call tells you who to ring. Those two should not be a scroll apart.

The honest cost, stated plainly: C scores worst on gaps — 13%, against 3% for what is built now. That white is real. It sits in band three, where Needs you is shorter than the band Who to call sets. I am recommending C anyway, because the two numbers measure different things: 13% of the grid gutters is white you scan past, and 49% of every line inside a card is white you read through. Last time I nearly recommended a layout on the gap figure alone and it was the emptiest of the six.

If you want the safest answer instead, take B. It is the same page height as what is built now, its gap figure is middling at 11%, and the reference column down the right is the calmest thing to look at. It just leaves Who to call where it already is.
