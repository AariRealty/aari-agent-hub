Three more, for the three cards

Three whole broker dashboards. The Leaderboard, Needs you and Your roster are drawn differently in every one — nine designs, none of them repeats of what you have already seen. Everything else is exactly what is built now and what you called good.

Version 1Tiles, days, and seats

Leaderboard becomes six small tiles — monogram, volume, files, and how long since that agent last closed anything. Needs you is ordered by how many days each thing has waited, oldest at the top, with the day count as the big number. Your roster is a grid of six seats, one dot each: green reaches them, red does not.

Version 2Who to call, and who is waiting

Leaderboard stops ranking by volume and ranks by days since the last closing, so it becomes a call list: Alied has not closed anything in 116 days and sits at the top, not the bottom. Needs you groups by who is actually holding it up — you, an agent, or nobody. Your roster splits into two plain columns: reachable, and not.

Version 3Said in words

Leaderboard becomes one stacked bar of the company’s volume, so you see that Milennys is 82% of everything the brokerage closed rather than reading four numbers and doing it in your head. Needs you drops counts and chips entirely and says each thing in a sentence. Your roster counts problems rather than people: four bounce, one has no row, one is suspended.

Which one

Version 2. It is the only one of the three where every card tells you to do something rather than telling you a number. A leaderboard sorted by volume tells you what you already know — Milennys is ahead. Sorted by days since the last closing it tells you something you cannot get anywhere else on the screen: Alied Machuca has closed nothing since 27 April, which is 116 days, and two agents have never closed at all. That is a list of calls to make.

Version 1 is the most scannable and the tiles are the nicest thing here to look at; take it if you want the screen to read quickly rather than to point at anything. Version 3 has the single most useful fact on it — that one agent is 82% of the brokerage, which is a concentration risk nothing else on your dashboard states — but a card made of sentences is slower to scan every morning, and Needs you loses its counts.

Every number is computed, not typed: the day counts come from the closing dates in your own rows against today, and the shares from the four volumes. Mix freely — the nine are independent.
