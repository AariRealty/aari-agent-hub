Three more ways to build Database

Drawn in the dashboard’s own cards, so what you see here is what the tab would look like. Real counts: 204 clients and 3 vendors, 202 people once the four households collapse, tiers A 22 · B 79 · C 101, and 198 who have never been contacted. Filters and buttons work.

Option AOne number and its levers

Reduces the whole page to a single score — how much of the book you are currently top of mind for — and then shows the three plays that would move it, each with the points it is worth and a button that starts it. The only layout that tells you what to do next rather than what is wrong. Right now the score is 1 out of 202, which is the honest number.

Option BTwo lists, nothing else

Owe a call on the left, everyone else on the right, and no third thing. No health bar, no tiers, no jobs. The least there is to look at, which is the point — you open it, you work the left column, you close it. The right column exists only so the book is still searchable.

Option CThe whole book at a glance

Every one of the 202 people is a dot, coloured by how the relationship is doing. Click any dot to pull that person up. It tells the truth faster than any number can — a wall of grey with four coloured dots in it is the state of the database, and no chart softens that.

Which one

I would pick A. Every other layout, including the three you liked, describes the database; A is the only one that hands you a next action with a number attached to it. The score also survives the thing that makes the others awkward — 192 of these people arrived in one import fifteen days ago, so "198 never contacted" is not really a failure yet, but "1 of 202 in cadence" is a fair measure from day one and it moves the moment you log a call.

B is the one to build if the page keeps getting ignored. Nothing on it can be browsed instead of worked. C is the most honest and the best thing to show yourself once a month, but it is not a daily page — once the grid is mostly green it stops earning its space, and until then it is 198 grey dots telling you the same thing every morning.
