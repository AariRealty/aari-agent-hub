The site does that with a named eyebrow and a hairline rule rather than with colour — its rule is black and white only. So the three below are three readings of your ask: the site’s own method, the same thing pushed into tone, and the same thing pushed into a full inversion. All three keep the type, the buttons and the watermark identical.

Why it works: the divisions are unmissable and it invents nothing — inversion is already the site’s own move, it is what every button does on hover. It is also the safest of the three in an inbox, because half the letter is white. Cost: four hard edges between black and white is a lot of contrast for something read on a phone at arm’s length, and the letter reads as louder than the site it came from.

One caution that comes with the black. A letter that is mostly a solid dark background is the hardest thing to land in an inbox — Outlook and several Gmail dark-mode combinations invert parts of it, and heavy dark blocks are the single biggest signal that pushes mail toward Promotions. Option C is the safest of the three on that count because half its area is white. If deliverability matters more than the match, say so and I will build a fourth that keeps the type and the buttons and inverts the ground.
