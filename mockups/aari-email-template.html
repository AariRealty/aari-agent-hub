A colour for every section

You were right on both counts. The letters were one flat colour top to bottom, and on a phone you could not find the other two — option C started four screens down a 4,777px page with nothing telling you it was there. There is now a picker at the top that shows one letter at a time. And every section below is its own band, in its own colour.

I could not reach joinaari.com to match it band for band. This machine routes through a proxy that blocks that host, so rather than guess at the site I built the runs out of Aari’s own colours — the ones in the Hub and in the repo’s own index.html: paper #fcfcfa, warm #faf5eb, blush #FFF8F4, stone #e7e2d5, taupe #c9bfa8 and the two darks. If you send me a screenshot of the site, or the order its bands run in, I will match it exactly.

One at a time, so none of them get buried.

Option AThe warm run Every band a different warm tone, lightest at the top, deepening as you read. One dark band at the end.

Why it works: it is the gentlest way to do what you asked. Nothing shouts, but no two sections touch in the same colour, so the eye knows where one idea stops and the next begins. It is also the safest in an inbox — five light bands render identically everywhere. Cost: the steps between the tones are small. On a dim phone screen in daylight, paper and blush are nearly the same, so part of the effect is lost exactly where most people read.

Why it works: you cannot miss the divisions. Each dark band frames the light one after it, and the section headings land on black where they carry furthest. It keeps the drama of the original without a drop of lime. Cost: three dark bands is a lot of dark. Outlook and some Gmail dark-mode combinations invert parts of dark blocks, and heavy dark backgrounds are the single biggest thing that pushes a message into Promotions.
