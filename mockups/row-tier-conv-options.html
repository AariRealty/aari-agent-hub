Tier and the Conv button

Three ways to get the tier off its own line and up beside the button, with a smaller button so both fit. All three are shown at phone width with your longest names in them — that is what decides this, not the short ones.

Why it drops to the bottom today

The row is four columns — badge, name, button, tier. On a phone there is not room for four, so the tier is pushed onto a second line under the name. It reads like a separate thing when it is really just a label on the row. Every option below keeps the row to three columns so that never happens.

Option ASide by side

Exactly what you described: tier chip, then the button, both on the right of the row. The button drops to 10px type and tighter padding so the pair fits on one line next to even a long name.

Everyone you know
Reads left to right the way you said it. The pair is the widest of the three, so the name column is 30px narrower and one more name wraps.
Option BStacked right

Same two pieces, but the tier sits directly under the button rather than beside it. Still one row, still together, and it gives the name column back 30px.

Everyone you know
Widest name column, and shorter overall than A — six rows come to 490px against A’s 512px.
Option CTier on the circle

The tier becomes a small badge on the corner of the initials circle, and the button sits alone on the right. The tier travels with the person rather than competing with the action.

Everyone you know
Shortest of the three — 460px for the same six rows, so about one extra person per screen. The badge is small, fine for A, B and C, tight for a future +A.

Which one

I would pick B. It is what you asked for — tier and button together on the right, button smaller — and stacking them rather than lining them up is what buys the name its room back. Measured at 390px on your six longest rows: A leaves 139px for the name and wraps three of six, B leaves 169px and wraps two. B is also the shorter of the two overall, 490px against A’s 512px, so it costs you nothing in scrolling.

A is the literal reading of what you said, and it works — but the chip has to shrink to just the letter to fit, and Arcela Gomez Carmona still wraps where she does not in B. C is measurably the best of the three, 460px for the same six rows, and I like where it puts the tier: on the person rather than beside the action. The reason I am not leading with it is that it moves the tier away from the button, which is the opposite of what you asked, and the corner badge is tight for the +A tier you want to add.

All three shrink the button the same way: 10px type instead of 11, and 6 by 11 padding instead of 7 by 14 — 65 by 32 pixels, still above the tap minimum. And in all three the tier stays in the right-hand column on a phone, never on its own line under the name.
