What becomes two taps, and what I would not touch

Four controls go from one tap to two

  • Sort tiers, All stages, All tiers, All types. All four move into the drawer.
  • Nothing else moves. Search keeps its own row. All seven health chips stay one tap, and they are the triage you actually use: All, Leaking, Slipping, Never touched, Healthy, Unworked, Snoozed, D.
  • The Filters control carries a count when any are set, so a filter left on is never invisible behind a closed drawer.

If you narrow by tier daily, say so and I will pull All tiers back out to sit beside Search as its own chip. That costs about 44px and still lands under 500. The other three I am confident about; tier is the one I would guess wrong on.

The counter can go because Today already prints it

__db.todayConvos and __td.todayConvos are the same figure computed twice from the same table. Today's banner says Your number today with the count; Database's hero says the same number with a different label. Removing it from Database loses nothing and returns 109px, on the screen where you browse rather than work.

Do not remove Log with no contact

It reaches 419px and I recommend against it. Log with no contact exists only on Database. Today has __tdLog for a named contact and the note and item of value paths, but no route for a conversation with someone who is not in your book. Removing the button removes the only door to it. If you want the 41px, the honest way is to move it up beside the tabs so it stops owning a row, which is a layout change I can make without touching Today.

So: is it filters, or is Database carrying the wrong screens?

Both, and you were right to make the test. Filters alone stops at 569px. What actually breaks 500 is removing a block that belongs to Today and was duplicated here.

Which points at the larger question you raised. On the evidence of this one screen, Database is carrying one thing that belongs elsewhere, not several: the counter. The rest of the header is genuinely Database's own work, browsing and narrowing a book of 207 people. That is a smaller answer than you may have expected, and I would rather say so than manufacture a reorganisation to match the question.

Approve the fold plus the counter removal and I will build both as one change, appearance and one deletion, no behaviour touched beyond the drawer's open and close.
