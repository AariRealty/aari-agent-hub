Your Monthly Edit, in Aari’s brand

I have the template now. The 43-page PDF opened, and the three Notes screenshots gave me the prompts for pages 4, 5 and 6. It is not the sales letter I have been building. It is a six-section monthly, and that changes what the Hub has to do. Below: what fills each section, the prompt that writes it, and three ways to lay it out.

Nothing local is invented here. Every event, business and deal slot is drawn as an empty slot, because I have no way to research Fort Myers from this session and guessing would put fake openings under your licence. Your own template says the same thing on page 5: verify every response, and it names where to check.
What the Hub can and cannot fill. It holds Aari’s own book: 7 active listings and 52 closed transactions. It does not hold Lee County market statistics. There is no MLS feed in the database, only your own deals. So the market snapshot’s four figures come from your MLS export, exactly as the template says. The events, the new businesses and the deals need research every month, and checking.

The six sections, and what writes each

The prompts keep the shape yours: a research instruction, a literal output format, a character limit, a worked example, and where to verify it.

1The Monthly Edit The Hub fills this

Your name, your city and the month. The Hub has all three.

2From the editor You write this

Six lines in your own voice, plus three captions for last month’s photographs.

Prompt 1Write my opening note for [MONTH] in Fort Myers. Warm, plain, no sales pitch. Mention one thing the brokerage actually did last month. Format: Four to six short lines. 480 characters or less. Happy February. If you are new here, this is where I put what is happening around town…
3Lee County market snapshot From your MLS

Four figures, each against last month, plus your read and the two invitations.

Not a promptThese four numbers come from the MLS, not from a model and not from the Hub. The Hub holds Aari’s own listings and closings. It does not hold Lee County market statistics. Format: Sold listings · Active listings · Average sold price · Average days on market Paste them from your MLS export. The source line prints underneath whatever you enter.
4New & coming soon Needs research & checking

Four or five openings, relocations or local real estate news.

Prompt 2Research 5 new businesses, restaurants, retailers or real estate news in Fort Myers. Format: Name, then what is happening. 70 characters or less each. Alessi Bakery: relocating to Cypress Street
5Events this month Needs research & checking

Ten or more, with a few that work for families.

Prompt 3Pull at least 10 events and concerts in or near Fort Myers for [MONTH]. Include a few kid-friendly ones. Format: Event name, then date | location underneath. Art Walk | Feb 14 | Downtown River District Verify every one. Check Facebook Events, Eventbrite, local tourism accounts, local news, and search “events in Fort Myers”.
6Local deals Needs research & checking

Five, plus your sign-off.

Prompt 4Research local discounts or deals running in Fort Myers this month. Think free museum days, happy hours, discounted admissions, specials at local businesses. Format: Deal or discount | one sentence. 150 characters or less. Edison & Ford Estates | Free admission for Lee County residents on the first Sunday

Why it works: it is the version that actually gets read on a phone between showings. Everything else lives behind one link, so the research is not wasted. It is just not all in the inbox. Cost: it needs somewhere to link to, which means the full edit has to be hosted, not just emailed. That is a build, not a template.

The honest cost of this format. Three of the six sections need fresh local research every single month, and all three have to be checked before they send. That is real work, call it an hour, and it does not shrink. Option C exists because it is the version that survives a busy month: one screen, and the rest behind a link when there is time to write it.
