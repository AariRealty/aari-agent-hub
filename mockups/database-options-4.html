Three more ways to build Database

Same rule as the first three — real rows, working filters, working conversation button. Counts are today’s: 204 clients and 3 vendors, 202 records once the 4 households collapse, tiers A 22 · B 79 · C 101, and 198 who have never been contacted. A, B and C answered who is going cold. These three answer why would I call, where do they live, and what do I do today.

Option DReasons to call

Sorts the book by the calendar instead of by neglect. Anniversaries are the one excuse to call that never feels like a sales call, and you already hold 33 dates — 23 home anniversaries and 12 wedding anniversaries. Two of them went by this month unmarked. The gap is the story: 174 people have no date on file at all, so nothing will ever surface them.

Option EYour farm

Puts the book on the map. 54 people — a quarter of everyone you know — live in Lehigh Acres, and 8 of them are tier A. That is a farm you already have and are not working. It also surfaces the thing that blocks every mailer: 107 records have no address.

Option FThe week, prewritten

Stops being a database and becomes a schedule. Seven days, three names already assigned to each, pulled A first. You never choose who to call — you open the day and it is decided. It also does the cadence maths out loud, which is uncomfortable: the tiers you set promise about 48 conversations a week, and three a day pays for 15.

Which one

I would pick F. You told me the point of this page is to complete your touches every day, and F is the only one of the six that answers that without you making a decision first — the names are already in the day. It is also the only one honest about the arithmetic: at three a day you touch 15 people a week against a book that asks for 48, so either the number goes up or tier A gets smaller. Better to see that now than to find it in March.

D is the one I would add second, and it is the cheapest win here — you are already holding 33 anniversaries and letting them pass. E is the most interesting and the least urgent: 54 people in Lehigh Acres is a real farm, but you cannot mail it until the 107 blank addresses are filled, so E is a project rather than a daily page.
