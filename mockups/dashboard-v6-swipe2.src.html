One thing at a time

These three are live, drag them, same as the last set. But they answer a different question. A, B and C were all about how to move between tabs. These are about not losing the thread while you move, which is the part that actually costs you.

What I designed against, so you can tell me if I aimed wrong. Three things, and none of them is decoration. Holding it in your head is the expensive part, so the screen should carry what you were doing rather than you. Starting is harder than continuing, so the next action should be one big obvious target, not a page to read and a decision to make. And coming back should be free, because the tax is not swiping away, it is the thirty seconds of working out where you were.

I cannot tell you these are ADHD approved and I am not going to pretend otherwise. They are built on those three ideas, that is all. You will know within about ten seconds of dragging each one whether it helps, and that is worth more than anything I could argue.

All three are mobile only, and none of them is in your Hub. Same as before.

D changes more than navigation. The other five options move you between tabs. D argues the tabs are the problem: on a morning when you have four things to do, a menu of five places to look is four decisions before you have done anything. It puts the jobs themselves in the deck and demotes the tabs to a menu you open when you want to go looking for something. That is a bigger change than you asked for, so I want to be plain that it is one.
