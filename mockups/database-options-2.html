Three more, built for staying top of mind

You said the job is to be top of mind, to finish your touches daily, and to see at a glance who is healthy and who needs you. These three are built for that rather than for browsing a list. Of the first set I would still say A, health first — but for a narrower reason than I gave you before. Now that the job is clear, the split is: Today is where you do the work, and Database is where you see whether the work is landing. A is the only one of those three that answers is my book warming up or cooling down in a single glance, which is the question Database should own.

B is really a planning screen — good once a quarter for deciding where to spend your time, not something you open daily. C duplicates Today; if Database also shows a worklist you end up with two to-do lists that disagree.

Option DThe wall

One square per person, coloured by relationship health. You see the whole book at once — and right now that is a wall of 199 grey squares, which is the most honest picture of where you stand. Every conversation you log turns one square green, so progress is visible from across the room. Click any square to see who it is.

Option ECadence ledger

Sorts by when someone is due rather than by tier. Tier A is weekly, B monthly, C quarterly, so every contact has a next-due date whether they know it or not. Overdue at the top, on track at the bottom. This is the one that finishes your touches, because it tells you who is owed a call today rather than who exists.

Option FTop of mind score

Turns the book into one number you are trying to move — the share of your database in a healthy relationship — with the four states beneath it and the people at each extreme. A scoreboard, not a list. Right now it reads 1%, which is the number that should make you open Today.

I would build E, the cadence ledger, and put F’s score as a single strip on top of it. Together they answer both halves of what you described: the score says whether you are winning, the ledger says who is owed a call right now. Neither duplicates Today — Today gives you three names and a number; Database tells you why those three and what is queued behind them.

D is the one to show an agent on day one. A wall of grey squares explains the problem faster than any number, and it is genuinely satisfying to watch it turn over. I would keep it as a small panel inside E rather than a screen of its own.
