Three scorecards for the people who paid you

All three sit behind a toggle on Database, so the everyday list stays what it was and this is a deliberate look. Every figure is real: 23 past clients, 33 paid files, $108,208 gross and $4.1M in volume, joined through realty_transactions.contact_id which already links 35 of the 56 rows.

Option AThe scorecard

Four numbers across the top, then every past client ranked by what they paid you, with how long they have been quiet on the right. The rank and the silence sit on the same line, which is the whole argument — your biggest client is also the one you have not called.

Option BThe money bars

The same people as bars, length by what they paid, colour by how cold they have gone. You do not read it, you see it — a long red bar is money that already trusted you and has not heard from you since.

Option CTwo panels

Who sent you business on the left, who closed with you on the right, each with its own number. The only one that answers both questions at once and keeps referrers from being buried under a longer list of past clients.

Which one

I would pick A. It is the one that makes the point without you having to work for it: Beatriz paid you $17,174 across three files and has been quiet 13 days; Olga paid $10,800 and has never been contacted at all. Twenty of your twenty-three past clients have no contact on record. A number that big next to a name that valuable is the motivation you asked for.

B is the prettier version of the same thing and reads faster once you know the list. C is the one to build when referrals are actually being recorded — today the left panel would have nobody in it, because no column stores who referred whom.
