The mobile header

Three ways to open the page on a phone. All three fix the same two things: the tabs, which are currently unreachable, and the amount of screen spent before any content.

The tabs are the real problem

On a 390px screen the tab strip is squeezed into 55 pixels between the logo and the role switch, holding 644 pixels of tabs. That is nine percent of them, and not one tab fits fully — the word “Dashboard” you can see is cut off at both ends. Team, Deals, Money, Announcements, Classes, Compliance and Ask are all reachable only by discovering that a 55px sliver scrolls sideways.

The second thing is spend: 241 of the first 844 pixels go on the bar, the greeting and the metrics before the first card starts. That is 29% of the opening screen.

Option ATabs get a row

The bar keeps the logo, the role switch and you. The tabs move to their own full-width row underneath, scrolling sideways with a fade at the edge so it is obvious there is more. Greeting drops to 22px, the three metrics become one line.

Four tabs visible at rest instead of none, the rest one swipe away. Buys almost no height back — the row costs what the greeting saved.
Option BTabs behind a name

The tab strip becomes one button showing where you are — Dashboard ▾ — and tapping it drops a list of all eight. No sideways scrolling anywhere, and the bar stays a single row.

All eight visible at once, and the shortest header of the three. Costs a tap every time you change tab.
Option CTabs at the bottom

The five you use go to a fixed bar at the bottom, where a thumb already is, with More for the rest. The top becomes just the logo, the role switch and you, so content starts almost immediately.

Content starts highest of the three and switching is one thumb tap. It is a phone pattern, so the desktop keeps its own nav and the two stop matching.

Which one

I would pick A. It is the smallest change that actually fixes the thing that is broken, and it keeps the phone and the desktop reading the same way — which is what you have asked for repeatedly over the last few rounds. Every tab becomes reachable with a swipe instead of nine percent of one being visible.

B gives the shortest header and shows all eight tabs at once, and it is the one to take if you would rather never scroll sideways. The cost is a tap on every tab change, which on a page you move around a lot adds up. C is the most phone-native and puts the tabs under your thumb, but it makes the phone and the desktop two different products, and it needs a decision about which five tabs earn a slot.

Be clear on what each actually buys. All three shrink the greeting from 30px and collapse the three metric pills into one line, but A spends that saving again on the tabs row — it opens at 232px against 241, so it is a reachability fix, not a space one. B opens at 193px and C at 183px, because neither gives the tabs a row of their own. If the height is what is bothering you, A is not the one.
