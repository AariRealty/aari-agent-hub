The desktop section row

Same question as the phone set, different room. A desktop has no thumb and 1,100px of width, so two of the three phone answers do not translate literally — they translate sideways. Each option below says which phone option it pairs with, so one number covers both screens. Click the sections inside any window.

Where it sits today, measured at 1440px

Bar ends at 57px. Greeting 75–122. Section row 132–186. Metrics 200–252. First card at 268px — a quarter of a 1000px window gone before any content.

The row is 54px tall and sits alone in the middle of that stack, touching nothing above or below it. On a wide screen that reads as a decorative divider rather than as the navigation it is.

The row becomes a second line of the top bar — hairline above and below, running the full width, sticky so it never scrolls out of reach. The two levels of navigation finally touch, and the greeting drops down to sit directly on your content. This is the option where the phone and the desktop stay identical in structure.

Which one I would take

Option 2, the rail. It is the only one of the three that costs no height, keeps all five section names readable as words, and still works the day Deals has ten sections instead of five — which it will, because you have been adding to these, not removing. It is also the honest desktop translation of the phone answer: sections sit next to the navigation, in whichever direction that screen has room.

The real cost is width. At 1440px the grid drops from three cards across to two, so you scroll a little more. On the pages that matter most — Roster, Files, Deadlines — those are tables and lists rather than three-across cards, so they lose nothing.

Option 1 is the one to take if what you want above all is the phone and desktop being the same product. Option 3 is the prettiest and the cheapest, and I would happily build it, but I would be building something that breaks the first time you resize the window narrow.

Say 1, 2 or 3. Whichever you pick I will build the matching phone half from the other page, so the two screens stay one product.
