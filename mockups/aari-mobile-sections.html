The sections on a phone

The rail works on a desktop because a wide screen has spare width. A phone does not, so the same list became a shelf on the tab bar — and on your screenshot it is sitting on top of the income card. Three ways to fix that. The desktop rail does not change in any of them.

What is actually wrong

The shelf is 37px of permanent, fixed height at the bottom of every screen. It never moves, so it always covers whatever card is underneath it — the page reserves room at the very end, but anything you scroll past passes behind it.

And it costs the same 37px on Today, where there are only two sections, as it does on Deals where there are five. Two words should not cost a band.

Option 1Gets out of the way

The shelf stays exactly where it is, but it slides away the moment you scroll down and comes straight back when you scroll up — the way a phone browser hides its address bar. It is never covering the card you are reading, and it is never more than a flick away.

Scroll the phone down, then back up
Nothing to learn, and reading is never obstructed. It moves, so it is not in a fixed place the way the desktop rail is.
Option 2Up by the logo

The row moves to the top, pinned under the logo bar, and stays there. Nothing is ever covered, because the page starts underneath it rather than running behind it. Sections at the top, tabs at the bottom — two fixed places, neither ever moving.

Scroll — the row does not move
Always visible, always the same spot, covers nothing. Costs 36px off the top of every screen, and puts the sections furthest from your thumb.
Option 3Out of the tab itself

No row on the screen at all. Tap the tab you are already in a second time and its sections open upward out of the bar, as a list. Tap one and it closes. The sections live inside the tab they belong to, which is the truest version of what the rail does.

Tap Today at the bottom — it is already active
Zero permanent height, nothing ever covered, and the biggest tap targets of the three. The sections are invisible until you ask, and double-tapping an active tab is a thing you would have to be told once.

Which one I would take

Option 1. It is the smallest change from what is already built and it fixes exactly the thing in your screenshot — the shelf stops covering the income card the moment you start reading, and it is back the instant you flick up. Everything you liked about option 2 on the desktop stays true: the sections sit with the tabs, under your thumb, in the same order.

Option 2 is the one to take if a thing that moves is worse for you than a thing that costs height. It never covers anything and it never shifts — but it also puts the sections at the far end of the screen from the tabs they belong to, which is the 674px problem we started with, in a smaller form.

Option 3 is the cleanest screen by a mile and the one that respects Today having only two sections. The honest risk is the one you have hit before: things you cannot see stop existing. Tapping an active tab to reveal more is not a gesture you would guess — though I would put a small mark on the active tab so the page tells you.

Say 1, 2 or 3. The desktop rail stays exactly as it is either way.
