Picking the primary

Three shapes for the control itself. All three do the same job — they set whose name the household shows under and whose number the call button dials — and all three sit in the Household section of the contact card. Under each one you can see the list line it produces, so the button and the result are on the same screen. Tap them; they all work.

Both households below are real. The Becs have one obvious answer — Rolando has no number on file. The Bushes are a coin toss: both reachable, both tier B, neither contacted.

Option ATwo names, one pill

The pair sits in a single toggle and the chosen one goes black — the same control as your Relationships / Business / Scorecard switch. One tap, always visible, and you can see both options without opening anything.

Option BTap the person

Everyone in the household is listed with their number, and a dot marks who is primary. You tap the person, not a button — and because it is a list it still works if a household ever has three or four people in it.

Option COne swap button

It states the answer as a sentence — who it is and why — with a single button underneath naming the other person. Nothing to compare: you either agree with what it says or you press the one button.

Household
One thing worth knowing. Every household in your book is a pair of two, so all three work today. If you ever link three people — parents and an adult child at one address — the pill in A gets cramped and C stops making sense, because “the other one” is no longer a single person. B is the only one that does not change shape.

Which one

I would pick B. It is the only one that shows you the fact you are actually deciding on — each person's phone number, right next to the dot — so on the Bushes you are choosing between two numbers rather than between two names. It also already reads like the rest of your card, and it is the one that survives a household of three.

A is the tidiest and matches a control you already use, but it shows names only, so on a coin-toss household you would have to remember which Bush you call. C is the least work when the answer is obvious — on the nine households where only one person has a number it is close to perfect — but on the Bushes it picks for you and asks you to notice.
