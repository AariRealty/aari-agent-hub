Three ways to build Ask

Dressed in the Now You Know language — the rule, the serif headline, the black header block, the policy badge and the cream callout. The worked answer is the same in all three and quotes real text from Manual §11.1 and §11.2. Everything is clickable.

Option 1The answer is a drop

Ask a question and the answer arrives as one of your cards — numbered, categorised, badged with the section it came from, callout and all. Every answer becomes publishable: if it reads well you push it to the weekly series without rewriting it.

Option 2The shelf, with a search bar

Leads with what you have already written. The weekly drops sit as a shelf of cards and the bar above searches them — tap a tile and it opens underneath. Answers come from the library rather than from a model, so nothing can be invented; if it is not written yet, it says so.

Now you know
Things nobody told you… until now
Short reads on how we run transactions at Aari. New ones drop weekly.
Every drop names its section. Nothing here is generated on the fly.
Option 3A conversation of drops

Keeps the back and forth — your question in an ink bubble, the answer as a compact drop card underneath. Best for follow-ups, since the thread holds context, and the card keeps each answer looking like something you wrote rather than something a chatbot said.

Ask me anything about the paperwork

Which one

I would pick 2. It is the only one that cannot make something up — an answer either exists as a drop you wrote, or the page says it has not been covered yet, which on ICA and licence questions is the right failure. It also means the work you already do weekly becomes the product. 1 is the best looking and turns every answer into something publishable, but it needs a model behind it before it is honest. 3 is the one to build once agents start asking follow-ups.
