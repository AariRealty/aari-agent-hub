Why it works: it spends width instead of height, which is the one thing a phone has going spare at the top of a page. The dark tile keeps a bit of contrast without a band across the screen, and the count and the target sit in the one place your thumb already reaches for. Cost: your name has to share its line, so on a longer name than yours it would wrap or the tile would squeeze. And the day’s number is now told twice, once in the tile and once in the first figure of the row, which is exactly the sort of duplication I have been taking out everywhere else.

My pick is A, and not by much. It is the only one that keeps every word you have today while still getting your name to the top of the size order, and if the numbers are wrong we change six values rather than rebuild the block. B saves nearly twice as much and I would take it in a moment if you tell me the $692 line has never once changed what you did that morning. If it has, keeping it loud is worth the pixels.
